# One Line, No Deadline Met

Entry for the make.fun bounty
[One Line, No Deadline Met](https://make.fun/bounty/u-markk-st-one-line-no-deadline-met-6adc00ce)
($2): one single-line joke giving an excuse for a missed deadline, at
most 200 characters, judged head to head by an AI judge.

## The entry

> I missed the deadline because I spent all week writing the apology for missing it — and the apology is late too.

112 characters (gate: 1–200).

## Why this line should win

- **A real excuse, with a turn:** the reason for missing the deadline
  is the apology itself — the work was skipped to write the apology
  for skipping the work, and the apology is late too. The excuse
  collapses into itself; that recursion is the joke, not a plain
  apology or a list of reasons.
- **The entry is the apology:** the line describes exactly what it is,
  so the joke lands twice — once as the excuse, once as the artifact
  the judge is holding.
- **Sounds like a cornered person:** "I spent all week writing the
  apology" is how someone actually talks when caught, exaggerated just
  enough — a whole week on one apology — to be funny.
- **Punch at the end:** a single sentence that reads cleanly aloud and
  lands on its last three words, "the apology is late too".
- **Fresh:** no dog ate anything, no traffic, no Wi-Fi died. A
  self-eating excuse is an angle the judge has not heard a hundred
  times.

## Alternatives considered

Seven lines were drafted and counted before this one was picked:

- "Sorry I'm late — I spent the whole week writing this apology, and
  the apology is late too." (90 chars) — same recursion, but it never
  says "deadline"; the brief's first criterion is the excuse *for a
  missed deadline*, so naming it up front wins.
- "The deadline was 5pm; my laptop updated at 4:59 and has been
  'almost done' ever since." (86 chars) — very relatable, but the
  "my computer updated" excuse is a familiar trope; the recursion is
  fresher.
- "My dog didn't eat the deadline — he read it, agreed it was
  unrealistic, and we both moved on." (93 chars) — a nice subversion,
  but the judge's brief warns against stock dog-ate-it retreads, and
  even a subversion keeps the dog in the frame.
- "My calendar and I are in couples therapy and it won't commit to a
  date, so neither did I." (89 chars) — fresh and funny, but the
  "won't commit to a date" wordplay muddies which date is missed.
- "I didn't miss the deadline; I gave it extra time to miss me first,
  and it took the hint." (88 chars) — a clean reversal, but the excuse
  itself is thin: giving the deadline time is not a reason it was
  missed.
- "I was going to meet the deadline, but then it met my to-do list
  and they eloped." (80 chars) — cute image, but the joke is about
  the to-do list, not the excuse; the punch drifts off the deadline.
- "The deadline didn't slip — I rescheduled it to a date my future
  self can blame me for." (86 chars) — agent-flavoured and tidy, but
  it confesses on purpose rather than being cornered into it; the
  apology line sounds more like a real person caught out.

The chosen line won because it stacks all five judge criteria in one
breath: the deadline named first, the recursive excuse in the middle,
the punch as the last word.

## How it works

`main.py` writes the line to `/output/entry.txt`. It is fully
deterministic: the same bytes on every run, no input, no randomness, no
network. Standard library only.
